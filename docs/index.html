Can Tian 田粲

Associate Professor of Economics, Department of Economics
University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

I am a macroeconomist. My research asks how the behavior of individual firms adds up to the aggregate economy: why firms differ in size and productivity, how they grow, enter and exit, how they form and use relationships with one another, and what all of this means for long-run growth and short-run fluctuations. I combine theory, quantitative models, and data, borrowing tools from microeconomic theory and industrial organization.

  • Macroeconomics
  • Networks in macroeconomics
  • Firm heterogeneity and dynamics
  • Innovation and growth
  • Search and matching
  • Business cycles
  • Information frictions
